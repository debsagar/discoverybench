## 1. A citable criterion for "theory A is an effective theory of world W in regime R"

**What sources say.**

- Active automata learning (Angluin's framework) formalizes exact identification of a target machine via a *minimally adequate teacher* answering **membership queries** ("what does the target do on this input") and **equivalence queries** ("is this hypothesis exactly the target; if not, give a counterexample"). The learner's hypothesis is provably correct only once no counterexample exists — i.e., it agrees with the target on literally everything reachable by any query, not just the ones asked. Source: Angluin-descended L* literature, e.g. the IISc course notes on "Learning Regular Sets from Queries and Counterexamples" (https://www.csa.iisc.ac.in/~deepakd/atc-2015/L_Star_Algo.pdf — SEARCH-SNIPPET ONLY, PDF fetch failed as text but title/author/topic confirmed via search) and the general survey framing in "Exactly Learning Regular Languages Using Membership and Equivalence Queries" (https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/james.worrell/DFA-learning.pdf). The Myhill–Nerode theorem is the formal handle: two states of the world are "the same" under theory A iff no future input sequence distinguishes their continuations; a minimal automaton is exactly the quotient by this indistinguishability relation.
- Reachability analysis for discrete systems (automata, timed automata) is **decidable** — unlike general hybrid/continuous systems, where exact reachable sets are provably incomputable. Source: reachability-analysis literature summarized in search results on "Reachability Verification for Stochastic Discrete-time Dynamical Systems" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09843) and general formal-methods framing: "reachability is decidable for discrete systems such as automata and timed automata, and consequently there exists a rich set of tools" for verifying them. This gives the formal object for "regime R": the **reachable set** of world-states/observations from a given class of interventions (RUN configurations, POKE edits) within a bounded number of steps or a bounded intervention budget.
- Effective field theory (EFT) gives the physics-side vocabulary directly, even though none of the search results found an EFT paper phrased for *discrete* systems specifically. "Matching" means: two theories valid at different scales must agree on physical observables computed in their overlapping regime of validity; matching fixes the coefficients of the effective (lower) theory from the full (higher) theory so that "one should match all... diagrams with external light particles." The validity boundary of an EFT is where a higher-order/omitted term becomes non-negligible; it "cannot be extracted from low-energy measurements alone without... a hypothesis about the UV theory." Source: "Matching in Nonrelativistic Effective Quantum Field Theories" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/9910364) and general EFT lecture notes (https://indico.in2p3.fr/event/22195/contributions/86017/attachments/59873/81148/eftlectures.pdf). **No source discusses EFT-style matching for automata or discrete lattice-gas systems explicitly** — the translation below is mine.

**My inference / proposed formal criterion [INFERENCE].** Combining the automata-learning notion of exact identification-by-queries with the EFT notion of matching-plus-validity-boundary, and using decidable reachability as the formal substrate:

> Let W be the true transition function (a Mealy/Moore machine on hidden state, or a full simulator) and A a candidate program. Let **Reach(R)** be the set of observation traces producible from *some* sequence of RUN/POKE interventions drawn from regime R (a bounded-budget, bounded-arity subset of all possible interventions — e.g. "at most k balls poked per cell, only in sparse configurations"). Then:
> **A is an effective theory of W in regime R** iff (i) for every trace t ∈ Reach(R), A(t) = W(t) (frame-by-frame observational equivalence on the reachable set — this is exactly the "no counterexample exists" condition from L*/MAT teachers, restricted to R), and (ii) there exists at least one trace t′ ∈ Reach(R′) for some R′ ⊋ R (a strictly larger, still-reachable regime — e.g. more crowding, more or sharper pokes) with A(t′) ≠ W(t′). Condition (i) is the *matching* condition (A and W's "physical observables" agree wherever both are probed); condition (ii) is the *validity boundary* (there is a reachable place where the match provably breaks, which is what makes R a genuine floor rather than a vacuous one — a theory that never breaks anywhere reachable isn't a "shallow theory," it's just correct).
>
> Decidability of reachability for discrete systems (cited above) is exactly what makes condition (i) checkable by exhaustive/BFS search over Reach(R) rather than needing continuous-system reachability tools — this is a load-bearing reason to keep the substrate discrete (grid, finite ball count per cell) rather than continuous.

## 2. When is hidden per-ball state identifiable from run + poke interactions? Minimum experiment, and scaling with number of hidden states

**What sources say.**

- HMM identifiability: the search results (via the JHU notes page, fetch of the PDF text failed but the search-engine summary reported specific numeric claims) state that "the distribution of an HMM with r hidden states and κ observed states is completely determined by the marginal distribution of **2r consecutive variables**," and a sharper result needs only **2r − 1 consecutive variables** — i.e. generic identifiability of the full HMM (transition + emission matrices) from the joint distribution of a window of about 2r observations, under a genericity/full-rank condition on the model. Source: http://www.econ2.jhu.edu/people/hu/HMM.pdf (SEARCH-SNIPPET ONLY, PDF fetch failed as text — this is the search engine's summary of the source, not my own read of the primary text; treat the "2r vs 2r−1" numbers as unverified until the PDF is read directly). Corroborating framing: "structural identifiability is a property of a given dynamical model and generically dependent on which model states are measured" and "Full Input-State-Parameter Observability (FISPO)... requires that all states of some auxiliary model are observable" — from "On the importance of structural identifiability for machine learning with partially observed dynamical systems" (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04131v1).
- Active automata learning (Mealy machines specifically — the closest formal analogue to "poke a ball, watch its output, its hidden state persists"): learning relies on three primitives — **reset**, **output query**, **equivalence query** — and the central technical difficulty when hidden state includes something like a clock/mode is **inferring resets**, which are "central to the dynamics... but not directly observable." Source: "Active Learning of Mealy Machines with Timers" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.02019) and "Learning Mealy Machines with One Timer" (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-68195-1_13). This is a precise structural analogue to the Floor-3 "hidden inner state that flips on certain collisions": a ball's hidden bit behaves like a Mealy-machine mode that is only revealed through *designed* transitions (collisions), not passive observation, and the learning algorithm's job is exactly to find inputs (poke sequences) that force a reset/reveal.

**My inference [INFERENCE], combining the two literatures for the ball-grid case:**
- A single ball's hidden state (say, a k-valued mode) is identifiable from run+poke interactions **only if** there exists at least one poke-and-collide sequence, reachable within budget, whose *output* (post-collision trajectory) differs across the k mode values — i.e., the k modes must be pairwise "Myhill–Nerode distinguishable" by some collision experiment. If two hidden modes never produce different observable collision outcomes under any reachable poke, they are not identifiable (this is a direct restatement of Myhill–Nerode indistinguishability, transposed from automata to physical state).
- Minimum experiment size, by analogy to the HMM result (2r−1 consecutive observations to identify r hidden states) and to Mealy-machine reset-inference (need a distinguishing sequence for each pair of states, i.e., roughly *k* pairwise distinguishing collisions in the worst case, or O(log k) if a single well-chosen adaptive sequence can binary-split the mode space — see §3): expect the number of designed collisions needed to scale at least linearly in the number of hidden per-ball states in the *non-adaptive/worst-case* regime, and possibly O(log k) adaptively if collisions can be chosen sequentially based on prior outcomes (this maps directly onto GBS, §3). **This scaling claim is my extrapolation, not something any source stated for a lattice-gas/ball system — no source addresses this substrate.**

## 3. Lower bounds on number of experiments to identify a rule among a hypothesis class — adaptive vs non-adaptive; computing the best adaptive planner and best fixed plan

**What sources say.**

- **Angluin's L\*** (query-complexity, DFA-learning, adaptive-by-construction since equivalence-query counterexamples steer the next round): learning an n-state target requires a number of membership + equivalence queries **polynomial in n and in the length of the longest counterexample returned**, and specifically (per the search-engine's paraphrase, since the primary PDF didn't render as text — SEARCH-SNIPPET ONLY) **O(n²m) membership queries and O(n) equivalence queries** where m is the longest counterexample length. Angluin's algorithm adds at least one state to its hypothesis per round, so the number of rounds is bounded by the size of the minimal automaton (Myhill–Nerode index). Sources: https://www.csa.iisc.ac.in/~deepakd/atc-2015/L_Star_Algo.pdf, https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/james.worrell/DFA-learning.pdf (both confirmed via search-engine summary; direct text fetch failed for the first).
- **Generalized Binary Search (Nowak, and Nowak & Dasarathy "Geometry of GBS," https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.4397)**: for a *noiseless*, adaptive setting, discriminating among |H| candidate hypotheses (the rule could be any one of them) requires **O(log|H|) adaptive queries** when the hypothesis-query pair satisfies a "neighborly" condition (each query roughly halves the surviving hypothesis set), and this is information-theoretically optimal — no adaptive strategy can beat Ω(log|H|) in the worst case, since each binary-outcome query carries at most 1 bit. A noise-tolerant variant achieves the same optimal asymptotic rate under a bounded-noise model. (Primary PDF fetch failed as text — SEARCH-SNIPPET ONLY, summary from search engine; I was not able to directly verify the neighborliness definition or exact theorem statement.)
- **Hanneke's theory of disagreement-based active learning** (https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~hanneke/docs/active-survey/active-survey.pdf, https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~hanneke/docs/2014/hanneke14a.pdf — confirmed via search-engine summary only, SEARCH-SNIPPET): introduces the **disagreement coefficient**, a distribution-dependent quantity controlling how fast the region of disagreement among plausible hypotheses shrinks as the version space shrinks; label/query complexity of active learning in the realizable case is characterized in terms of this coefficient, giving a general (non-GBS-specific) framework for adaptive sample complexity bounds — this is the right tool if the ball-grid hypothesis class isn't as clean as GBS's binary-split structure.
- **Improper learning / agnostic PAC** results (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03871 "Agnostic Learning by Refuting", https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19777 "Revisiting Agnostic PAC Learning"): establish that lower bounds on (even improper) learning reduce to lower bounds on an associated *refutation* problem, and separate proper vs. improper learners by their achievable sample complexity — relevant to §4 below since a "hypothesis-class boundary" argument is precisely a refutation-style lower bound.
- **Bayesian experimental design for model discrimination** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.05301 "Optimal Bayesian design for model discrimination via classification", https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07949 "Metrics for Bayesian Optimal Experiment Design under Model Misspecification"): the standard design criterion for choosing which experiment to run next when discriminating between candidate models is the **mutual information between the model indicator and the (not-yet-observed) data**, i.e., expected information gain (EIG); recent extensions add "Expected General Information Gain" and "Expected Discriminatory Information" specifically to handle model *misspecification* (the true model isn't in the candidate set) — directly relevant to a benchmark where the agent's hypothesis class may not contain the true floor-3 rule.

**My synthesis [mixing sourced facts + inference]:**
- **Best fixed (non-adaptive) plan** = choose a batch of interventions in advance to maximize worst-case (or expected, if Bayesian) distinguishing power over the hypothesis class; computing it is a combinatorial/experimental-design optimization — the EIG-maximization criterion from Bayesian experimental design is the natural objective, computed offline by simulating each candidate rule against each candidate intervention set (sourced idea, but "compute it this way for our benchmark" is my inference).
- **Best adaptive planner** = a decision tree over interventions, choosing the next poke based on all previous outcomes; GBS gives the exact answer (O(log|H|) queries, optimal) when the hypothesis class is amenable to near-balanced splits, and Hanneke's disagreement-coefficient framework generalizes this when it isn't. The gap between best-fixed and best-adaptive-plan query counts is itself a measurable, reportable number for the benchmark (adaptive is worst-case exponentially better for pathological classes, per GBS/Hanneke theory), and matches the paper's "recovery after failed submit" metric conceptually: a good agent should behave like a GBS-style adaptive planner, not a fixed-batch tester.

## 4. Certifying that a shallow theory CANNOT be patched to fit the deep regime (hypothesis-class separation)

**What sources say.**

- No source directly addresses "certifying non-patchability" for physical/automaton rule classes as a named concept. The closest formal tools found:
  - **Refutation-based lower bounds in agnostic/improper learning** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03871): a lower bound on learnability reduces to showing a *refutation* problem is hard — i.e., showing that no efficient procedure can certify "no hypothesis in class H fits this data" when in fact none does. Turned around, this gives exactly the right shape of statement: **to certify shallow theory A cannot be patched (i.e., no member of A's hypothesis class, including any patched/parameterized version, fits the deep-regime data), exhibit a finite, reachable dataset (a set of poke outcomes) that refutes every member of A's hypothesis class simultaneously** — a finite witness of separation, not a statistical argument. Source: refutation-learning line of work summarized above.
  - **Myhill–Nerode style distinguishing sequences** (from the automata-learning sources, §1–2): if the "shallow theory" corresponds to a coarser automaton (a quotient with fewer states/parameters than the true machine), any patch of it that only adjusts existing transition labels/outputs but does not add states cannot represent the finer partition that the deep regime requires. Certification = exhibit an input (a poke/collision sequence) that the coarse automaton, under *every* possible parameter setting within its class, cannot reproduce, because the two deep-regime behaviors it needs to distinguish were already merged into one shallow-theory state at construction time. This is a state-counting/VC-dimension-style argument.
- **My proposed operational procedure for the benchmark [INFERENCE, not sourced]:** run the shallow theory's hypothesis class through the same GBS/EIG-style experimental design as in §3, but treat "does any patch of shallow-theory-class fit" as the hypothesis-selection problem itself; if the best member of the shallow class still disagrees with the true world on a poke sequence reachable within the stated intervention budget, and this holds for *every* parameter setting (checked either exhaustively for finite parameter spaces, or via the refutation argument for infinite ones), that is the certificate. This is effectively re-using the §1 criterion's "condition (ii)" (a reachable disagreeing trace) but strengthened to quantify over the whole hypothesis class rather than one fitted instance.

## 5. Difficulty knobs that moved LLM performance on automata-learning benchmarks

**Source: "Can LLM Agents Infer World Models? Evidence from Agentic Automata Learning," arXiv:2606.16576, Menaged, Lior, Ravfogel, Aharoni, Stanovsky, submitted June 15, 2026** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16576, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16576 — abstract fetched directly; body fetched and parsed as text successfully, so these are higher-confidence than the PDF-fetch failures above).

Setup: agents must uncover a hidden DFA via membership queries (does this string belong to the target language) and equivalence queries (propose a candidate DFA; oracle confirms or returns a counterexample). Classic algorithms (L*, TTT) and their query counts serve as baselines/budgets.

Difficulty knobs that measurably moved performance, in the paper's own findings:
1. **DFA size (number of states)** — the dominant knob. Performance "drops sharply" as states increase from 2–3 to 8–9; no evaluated model exceeded 25% success at 8–9 states, versus 100% for the classical algorithms at the same sizes.
2. **Reasoning vs. non-reasoning models** — a large, discrete effect: at 4–5 states, a reasoning model (reported as "Gemini 3.1 Pro") reached ~85% success while several non-reasoning models (reported as "GPT-5.4 without reasoning," "Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite," "Llama-3.3-70B") reached 0%.
3. **Query budget** — set to roughly 2× the number of queries the better of L*/TTT would need; raising the budget helped some models substantially (one model, "Deepseek-V4-Pro," improved from 0% to 55% success) — i.e., budget alone is a real difficulty knob, not just automaton size.
4. **Query efficiency / non-informative queries** — even capable models degrade over the course of an episode: after ~60 interaction steps, a strong model issued non-informative queries ~20% of the time, and the best model needed ~45.8% more queries than the classical TTT algorithm on its successful runs. This is evidence for a "recovery after near-failure" style metric being genuinely diagnostic, matching this project's stated second metric.
5. **Alphabet size** was held fixed (binary) in their experiments and not reported as a manipulated knob — i.e., this is a knob the paper did *not* explore, worth flagging as a gap rather than a finding.

Failure modes reported qualitatively: "recurring failures in query planning, evidence integration, and hypothesis construction" — i.e., models struggle not at raw language-membership reasoning but at the *active experimental design* step (choosing which query to ask next) and at *integrating* prior query results into a consistent hypothesis, which is exactly the "best adaptive planner" computation in §3.

## Design recommendations for the ball-grid benchmark, traceable to sources above

1. **Define each floor's validity boundary as a reachability statement, not a vibe.** Use §1's two-part criterion (agree on Reach(R), disagree on some point of Reach(R′) ⊋ R) as the literal spec for "Floor k reduces to Floor k−1 in calm conditions." Because reachability is decidable for discrete automata/lattice systems (source: reachability-analysis literature, §1), this can be checked exhaustively by simulation rather than requiring continuous-system approximation — a concrete argument for keeping the substrate discrete, which the current design already does.
2. **Design Floor-3's hidden-state reveal explicitly as a Myhill–Nerode distinguishing-sequence problem** (§2): before finalizing which collisions flip the hidden bit, check that some *reachable* (within stated poke budget) collision sequence produces different observable outputs for each pair of hidden-mode values. If no such sequence is reachable within budget, Floor 3 is unidentifiable by construction, which would silently make the benchmark's headline "solved" metric meaningless for that floor. This is a concrete pre-launch test to run on the simulator, not just a design principle.
3. **Report both a non-adaptive and an adaptive query-complexity number per world**, using GBS-style reasoning (§3): compute (via brute-force simulation over the intervention space) the best fixed poke-batch's worst-case distinguishing power and the best adaptive planner's expected/worst-case query count. The gap between these two numbers is a designed difficulty knob independent of DFA-size-style knobs, and it lets the paper report "how much does adaptivity help" as its own axis — something the LLM-automata paper (§5) shows current agents are bad at (poor query planning / evidence integration), so it is likely to be discriminating.
4. **Borrow arXiv:2606.16576's knobs directly and adapt them**: state-count analogue = number of distinct hidden-state configurations per floor (crowding density for Floor 2, hidden-mode count for Floor 3); query budget as an explicit multiple of the optimal adaptive planner's count (§3); and track "fraction of non-informative pokes over an episode" as a diagnostic, since it was shown to degrade even strong models over time. Do **not** assume alphabet-size-style knobs (grid size, ball speed range) transfer performance effects — the cited paper did not vary that axis, so there's no evidence either way; flag this as untested rather than assuming it behaves like DFA size.
5. **Certify "cannot be patched" per §4 as a released, checkable artifact per world**: for each Floor-(k) truth and Floor-(k−1) hypothesis class, ship (in the world's ground truth, not shown to the agent) a specific poke sequence that refutes every parameter setting of the shallower theory. This turns the informal "not fixable by fudging the shallower theory" design rule into a testable pre-registration: if no such sequence exists for a candidate world, that world fails an internal QA check before release.

- No source in this sweep addresses identifiability or query complexity for a lattice-gas/cellular-automaton-with-hidden-per-particle-state substrate specifically.
